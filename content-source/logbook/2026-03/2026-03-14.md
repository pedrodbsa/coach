**Warm-up checkpoint (after strides, before rep 1):**
- **GREEN (proceed with 4 reps):** Legs feel responsive, strides were smooth, HR settled below 140 within 60s of stopping. RPE during strides ≤5.
- **AMBER (reduce to 3 reps):** Legs feel flat, strides were sluggish, or you needed >90s to get HR below 140. Do 3×2000m and call it.
- **RED (abort to easy run):** Heavy legs, unusual fatigue, HR >145 during warm-up jog, or RPE >6 during strides. Jog 5km easy and go home. Protect tomorrow.

**Execution notes:**

- **This is HM goal pace.** 5:15-5:25/km = sub-2:00 HM. It should feel like "I could hold this for another 30 min." If it feels like race effort, you're too fast.
- **Pacing discipline:** Your 10K race taught you what happens when you go out too fast in heat. Apply that lesson — start rep 1 at 5:25, let pace come to you.
- **HR will drift.** Rep 1 may finish at 160, rep 4 at 172. That's normal cardiac drift over 8km of quality. If rep 1 is already at 170+, the body isn't ready — drop to 3 reps.
- **Recovery jogs are 60 sec.** Short — this mimics race conditions where you don't get rest. If you need >90s to get HR below 145, extend the recovery but count that as an amber flag.
- **60s recovery is tight.** Walk the first 15-20s, then easy shuffle. HR won't fully recover — that's intentional.
- **RPE target:** 6-7 per rep. Overall session ~7. You should finish thinking "I had one more rep in me."
- **Talk test:** Short phrases during reps. If full sentences, speed up 5s/km. If single words only, slow down.

**Why 4 reps, not 5-6:** Sleep was short (6.4h), sensor data is suspect, and tomorrow is the peak long run. 4×2000m = 8km of quality — enough race-specific stimulus without burying you. If rep 4 feels easy, don't add a 5th. Bank the freshness for tomorrow.
