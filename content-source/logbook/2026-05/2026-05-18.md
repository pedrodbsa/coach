## The Sleep Problem — let's actually diagnose it

You said rough sleep, waking up, struggling to fall asleep, "maybe stress." Your clarification sharpens this: **you were in bed at 23:00 and didn't fall asleep until ~01:18.** That is not a late-bedtime problem — it is **~2 h 20 min of sleep-onset latency.** That single fact reframes the diagnosis. Two distinct failure modes are showing up:

- **Sleep-onset latency** — lying in bed awake for 2+ hours. This is the textbook signature of **pre-sleep arousal**: a mind or body that won't downshift. Stress and rumination are the classic driver; last night, post-race adrenaline stacked on top.
- **Fragmentation** — the night before, 8 h in bed produced 76 min awake across 4 wakeups. That's the "keep waking up" half.

Likely contributors, ranked by what the data now supports:

1. **Pre-sleep arousal — stress/rumination.** A 2 h 20 min onset latency is the strongest finger-print here. Your instinct that stress is involved is **probably right** — onset insomnia is exactly how stress presents. This is the main thread to pull.
2. **Post-race arousal.** Last night specifically: a near-max race spikes cortisol and sympathetic tone for 12-24 h, which directly delays onset. Some of last night's 2 h 20 min is the race — don't over-weight one post-race night.
3. **Caffeine.** Unknown — you don't currently log it. Race-day caffeine was ~230 mg, ~5-6 h half-life. An afternoon cup delays onset directly. We need data here before ruling it in or out.
4. **Screens / wind-down.** If the 23:00-01:18 window was spent on a phone or screen, that alone can produce this latency. What you do in that 2 hours matters.

On the **"not doing strength training"** theory — see the answer below. Short version: missing lifts is unlikely to be a direct sleep cause, and the fix is not a session today.

### What to do tonight and this week

Because the problem is **onset latency**, the front-line fix is **stimulus control** — retraining your brain that bed means sleep, not lying awake.

- **Stimulus control — the key one.** If you're not asleep in ~20-25 min, **get out of bed.** Go somewhere dim, do something boring and non-stimulating (read a dull book on paper, not a screen), return only when genuinely sleepy. Repeat as many times as needed. Lying in bed awake for 2 hours actively trains insomnia — the bed becomes associated with frustration and wakefulness. This feels counterproductive; it is the single most effective behavioral treatment for onset latency.
- **Wind-down buffer.** A real 45-60 min screens-off ramp before bed. The 23:00-01:18 window needs to be dim and low-stimulation, not phone time. If stress/rumination is the driver, this is where you intercept it — try writing tomorrow's worries/to-dos down on paper to get them out of your head.
- **Fix your wake time.** One wake time, held 7/7 including weekends, even after a bad night. Suggest **06:30-07:00**. This anchors the rhythm and builds sleep pressure for the next night.
- **Hard caffeine cutoff 12:00**, cap the total. Log every cup with its time starting today.
- **Morning daylight within 30-60 min of waking** — 10-15 min outside, on today's walk.
- **Mg glycinate 400 mg pre-bed** — already in your stack, keep it.

Do **not** chase a bad night by going to bed extra early the next evening — that lengthens time-in-bed-awake and makes onset latency worse. Hold the wake time, let sleep pressure build.
