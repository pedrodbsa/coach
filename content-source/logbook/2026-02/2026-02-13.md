## Coaching Note: Are Declining Predictions Caused by Missing Speed Work?

**Short answer: No. The main cause is gray-zone long runs, not the absence of speed sessions.**

Here's the full race prediction trend:

| Date   | 5K    | 10K   | HM      | Marathon |
| ------ | ----- | ----- | ------- | -------- |
| Feb 2  | 22:12 | 47:22 | 1:47:51 | 4:00:52  |
| Feb 10 | 22:19 | 47:37 | 1:48:27 | 4:02:07  |
| Feb 13 | 22:25 | 47:49 | 1:48:54 | 4:02:55  |

That's -13 sec (5K) and -63 sec (HM) in 11 days. But look at _what you've actually been running_:

| Date   | Run Type   | Avg HR  | MAF (142) | Verdict                  |
| ------ | ---------- | ------- | --------- | ------------------------ |

| Feb 10 | Base 5km   | 144     | 142       | Over, 176 max spike      |
| Feb 11 | Base 6km   | **140** | 142       | Compliant ✓ — progress!  |

**The pattern is clear:** Your base runs are improving (Feb 11 at 140 avg was your first truly compliant mid-week run in this block). But your long runs at 162-168 avg HR are chronically fatiguing. Garmin's algorithm calculates efficiency (pace ÷ HR) from recent runs. Running 6:01/km at 162 bpm = low efficiency = worse prediction. This has nothing to do with missing speed sessions.

**Why speed work wouldn't help right now:**

- Your system is carrying fatigue from gray-zone long runs
- Adding intensity on top of a fatigued base → injury risk or burnout
- The aerobic engine you're building takes 8-12 weeks to show in predictions
- Garmin predictions respond to recent _efficiency_, not training stimulus

**What WILL help:**

1. **Sunday's long run under 145 avg HR.** This is the single biggest lever. Walk uphills. Slow down earlier. A 13km run at 145 avg HR will improve your predictions more than a tempo session would.
2. **Continue MAF discipline on base runs** — Feb 11 at 140 shows you can do it.
3. **Quality work starts Feb 23** (9 days away) — sub-threshold intervals as planned.

**One concession:** I'm adding 6 strides at the end of today's run. These are 20-second neuromuscular activations, not speed sessions. They cost almost zero fatigue, maintain running economy, and begin preparing your legs for quality work next week. Think of them as "activation," not training.
